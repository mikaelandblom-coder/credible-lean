Credibility lab

Motor insurance: claim cost per vehicle-year by region, relative to the tariff (1.00). Choose the world, fit a model from the data alone, and compare it with a reference and with the best possible.

World (the truth)

Use it: with many claims per region and year (the default ~55) heavy tails barely matter: the sum smooths them. With few claims (try 50 vehicle-years) fits with region-specific variances (corrBS, BLUP) lose most and under-price the portfolio, since a big claim raises a region's mean and its σ̂ⱼ² together. Then prefer pooled variances (BS, BLUP pooled) or keep “Balance the total premium” on. Watch out: the typical error of one draw reads 15–25% low at α near 2 (right on average). Seen in simulation (studies/runs/lomax-tails.js), not proved.

Use it: “BLUP groups” splits τ² into the shared part φτ² and the regional part from pairs of regions within and across groups (one GLS pass, no tuning), and keeps the groups only when they win on held-out years. Watch out: with 2–4 groups φτ² is barely identified (its spread is 2–9 times its value), so the choice “groups or none” matters more than the weights; with a small φ and short histories no groups can be better (studies S69).

Models (see only the data)

corrBS (the default reference): Bühlmann–Straub with one correlation ρ̂ and region variances shrunk towards the pooled one by their own credibility; every parameter by a moment equation, no damping, nothing to tune, K̂ ≻ 0 by a theorem. “BLUP” plugs corrBS’s K̂ into the best linear unbiased predictor, which uses every other region individually instead of only through μ̂. “BLUP pooled” uses one noise variance for all regions: with the balance switch, two remedies for the under-pricing that region-specific variances cause under skewed claims (Finding 35; see Bias under Many draws). Bühlmann–Straub, own and pool are balanced already. “corr” uses the thesis estimators (37)–(41) with a damped K̂; “corr taper” tapers it by ρ̂|i−j| for regions in an order, ρ̂ from the data, and “BLUP taper” plugs that K̂ into the BLUP: worth it with many regions whose correlation fades with distance. “BLUP auto” tapers only when the data show the correlation falling with distance. “corrBS g/p” keeps region variances only when their exact gain beats their price (see Gain against price below); otherwise one pooled variance. “common” (one covariance for all pairs) is not recommended: with unequal volumes its z explode. Unequal lengths and group levels come next.

Monthly cohorts (a common factor and its fit)

A second world: cohorts observed monthly. Each cohort has its own mean per calendar month (the season); on top, a common AR(1) factor with loadings λⱼ (covariance γλᵢλⱼφ|t−s|), a shock C within each month, process noise sⱼ²/wⱼₜ and a cohort level τ². The fit sees ratios and volumes only: first φ̂, then MINQUE for γλλᵀ, C and s² given φ̂. τ² is absorbed by the season and not estimated (Finding 27).

External index: a public series that follows the same factor (e.g. travel demand), with its own season and noise, over more years than the data. The fit can take φ from it.

Comparing fits on data (rolling origin)

How a student would choose between two fits on the real data, checked here where the truth is known. At every month t₀ after a burn-in, each fit is refitted on the months before t₀ only (φ̂, tables, season), predicts each cohort's mean over the next 12 months, and is scored by the squared error, volume-weighted over cohorts. The two fits are compared by the difference of their losses on the same targets (the target year's shock hits both alike). Consecutive origins share 11 of their 12 target months, so the differences are autocorrelated: Diebold–Mariano with a long-run variance up to lag 11 and the Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold correction against t(n−1); also the weighted-periodogram version (Coroneo & Iacone). Same world as above (cohorts, φ, volumes, index), with a longer history. Not K-fold: the common factor ties held-out months to training months.

Crossed levels (rows × columns: country × car type)

A third world: a table of cells, each with a row (country) and a column (car type). A cell's level is the tariff plus a row effect (spread α), a column effect (β) and its own effect (γ), so two cells in the same row share α², in the same column β² (L = α²[same row] + β²[same column] + γ²I). On top, the thesis noise σ²/w with correlation ρ between cells in a year. The fit sees each cell's row, column, volumes and ratios: it estimates α², β², γ² from pairs of cells, and predicts every cell from its row and column too. Against it, the thesis fit, which sees the cells but not the table.
